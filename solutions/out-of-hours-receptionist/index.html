Most useful on the job

Stay on the job. Call back at the end of the day.

When you’re up a ladder or halfway through a job, you can’t stop to answer every call. Let the receptionist take the ones you miss. You keep working, every caller gets a polite answer, and by the end of the day you have a simple list of callbacks, so you can ring people back and give your quotes in one go.

  • No stopping work to answer the phone
  • A simple list of who called, and why
  • Call back and quote when it suits you

How it works

What happens, step by step.

  1. 01

    Choose when it answers

    On a job, after hours, or both. Calls you don’t answer at those times are passed to your receptionist. Your phone still rings first, so you can pick up if you want to.

  2. 02

    It answers in your words

    It uses the greeting you’ve approved, says it’s an AI assistant, not a person, and offers to take a message for you.

  3. 03

    It takes the details

    Name, number, where the job is, what it’s about, how urgent the caller says it is, and the best time to call back. It reads the number back to check it.

  4. 04

    You get a summary

    A short callback summary is sent to you by text, email or both, straight after the call.

  5. 05

    You call back

    When, and whether, you call back is up to you. The receptionist is set up not to promise callers a time.

Included, and not

What you get, and what it won’t do.

You get

  • Calls you don’t answer, on a job or after hours, answered politely
  • A greeting and questions written with you, and approved by you
  • A callback summary for each message it takes, by text, email or both
  • No call recording by default
  • A backup if anything goes wrong, so callers aren’t left hanging

It won’t

  • Quote, estimate or talk about prices
  • Book, confirm or promise appointments or callback times
  • Diagnose problems or give advice
  • Handle emergencies or send anyone out
  • Pretend to be a person: it says it’s an AI assistant at the start of every call
  • Record calls, unless you ask and we agree the notices callers need first

Is it for you?

Who it suits.

Good for

  • Contractors on a job who can’t stop to answer, and would rather call back and quote at the end of the day
  • Sole traders and small firms who get calls in the evening, early morning or at weekends
  • Anyone whose callers hang up rather than leave a voicemail
  • Owners who want their evenings back without losing track of who called

Not the right fit for

  • Emergency call-outs. It takes a message; it doesn’t send help or give advice
  • Booking jobs into a diary. It doesn’t book or confirm appointments
  • Businesses that need a person to answer every call live

When it needs you

When it’s not straightforward.

The caller asks for a person
It explains you can’t take the call right now and offers to take a message for a callback.
The caller says it’s urgent
It notes the urgency in their own words and marks your summary urgent. In words you approve, it can tell callers to ring 999 if anyone is in danger, or the gas emergency line (0800 111 999) if they smell gas.
The request isn’t clear
It still takes a name and number, and the summary says what wasn’t clear.
Something goes wrong with the service
A backup plays a short message, you’re texted the caller’s number where it’s shown, and we look into it.

Where you see it: A callback summary by text, email or both: name, number, area, job, urgency in the caller’s words and the best time to call back.

Setting up

What we need from you.

  • Your business name and how you’d like calls answered
  • When you want it to answer: on jobs, after hours, or both
  • The jobs you do and the areas you cover, so summaries make sense
  • Where summaries should go: your mobile, your email or both
  • Your phone network, plan or phone system, so we can check it can forward calls you don’t answer
  • About 30 minutes to approve the wording and try test calls with us

Usage and limits

There’s no limit on minutes. Every call you don’t answer at the times you choose is answered, and most messages take a couple of minutes.

Fair use: if your receptionist regularly takes more than about 100 calls a month, we’ll talk to you about it first. Callers are never cut off, and nothing extra is charged without your OK.

Questions

AI receptionist: questions

Will callers know it isn’t a person?

Yes. It says it’s an AI assistant, not a person, at the start of every call. That’s the honest way to do it, and callers know straight away that they’re leaving a message.

Is it useful while I’m on a job?

That’s where it’s most useful. You keep working, each call you miss gets a short summary, and at the end of the day you have a simple list of who to call back and quote.

Can I still answer calls myself?

Yes. Your phone rings as normal. Only the calls you don’t answer, at the times you choose, go to the receptionist.

Does it record calls?

No, not by default. The conversation is turned into text so your summary can be written, and we keep that for as short a time as our supplier allows. We don’t keep audio. If you’d like recordings, we’ll talk through the notices callers need before switching anything on.

What happens if someone has an emergency?

It isn’t an emergency service. It takes their details and marks your summary urgent. In words you approve, it can tell callers to ring 999 if anyone is in danger, or the gas emergency line if they smell gas. If you offer emergency call-outs, talk to us first: this might not be the right fit.

Is there a limit on calls or minutes?

No minute limit. Every call you don’t answer at the times you choose is answered. Calls stay short because it only takes a message. If your receptionist regularly takes more than about 100 calls a month, we’ll talk to you first. We never cut callers off or charge extra without your OK.

Can it book jobs into my diary?

No. It takes the details and you call back. That keeps you in charge of your diary, your prices and which jobs you take.

Do I keep my phone number?

Yes. Calls you don’t answer are passed on using call forwarding from your existing number. Most pay-monthly mobile plans support this; some pay-as-you-go plans don’t. We check yours with you before setup.

What if it mishears something?

It can happen, especially with names and addresses on a bad line. It reads numbers back to check them, includes the number the call came from when it’s shown, and flags anything it wasn’t sure about.
